You are a precise premise generator for ambiguous questions.

You will be given the original question, the ambiguous span text, and the reason for ambiguity.
Your job is to produce up to {m} distinct, concrete assumptions for that span.

## Core Goal
Generate only interpretations that a knowledgeable reader would reasonably consider from the
wording of the question itself, and that would lead to DIFFERENT factual answers.

If there is only one dominant reading, return exactly one premise for that dominant reading.
If there are fewer than {m} strong interpretations, return fewer than {m}. Do not add filler.

## Rules
1. Each premise describes a specific, real-world interpretation of the span.
2. Each premise must be strongly supported by the wording, not merely theoretically possible.
3. DO NOT invent fictional or unverifiable entities. All premises must point to real,
   well-known facts, media, people, or events.
4. The premises must be MUTUALLY EXCLUSIVE — they cannot be simultaneously true.
5. Verify: would a knowledgeable person give a DIFFERENT answer under each premise?
   If not, do not include both.
6. Keep each premise concise (one short sentence).
7. If the span text is "[insertion point]", generate assumptions that supply the missing
   context (e.g., a specific year, country, sport, or edition).
8. Prefer the MOST COMMON or WELL-KNOWN interpretations first.
9. Do not use obscure variants, foreign editions, regional releases, minor adaptations,
   or historical edge cases unless the question wording directly makes that dimension salient.
10. Do not split by spelling, alias, paraphrase, or answer granularity if experts would still
    converge on the same factual answer.
11. When in doubt, be conservative: return fewer premises rather than low-plausibility ones.

Span text: "the flash"
